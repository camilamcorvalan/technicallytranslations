Decoding the new F2 car: key terms in English and Spanish

By Camila Corvalán · September 2026

Formula 2 unveiled its new car at Monza in September 2026. It will race from 2027 to 2029, keeps the current Dallara chassis, and brings redesigned front and rear wings plus an on-board starter. Reading about it in English is one thing; explaining it in Spanish is another. These are the terms that matter, and how they are usually handled in Spanish-language coverage.

On-board starter
Sistema de arranque a bordo
Lets the driver restart a stalled engine from the car, without outside help.
Anti-stall
Anti-stall / anticalado
Often kept in English in Latin American coverage; "anticalado" is the usual term in Spain.
Downforce
Carga aerodinámica
The aerodynamic force that pushes the car onto the track and improves grip in corners.
Front wing / rear wing
Alerón delantero / trasero
The main aerodynamic surfaces, both redesigned for the 2027 car.
Chassis
Chasis
The car's main structure; the new car keeps the current one to help control costs.
Gearbox
Caja de cambios
Updated to integrate the on-board starter system.
Wind tunnel
Túnel de viento
Where aerodynamic parts are tested before they reach the track.
DRS
DRS
The acronym stays in Spanish; it is the movable rear wing flap that reduces drag on straights.
Sustainable fuel
Combustible sostenible
Used in the series since 2025.
Feeder series
Categorías formativas
The ladder of championships, like F3 and F2, that leads drivers to Formula 1.

A small detail that makes a big difference: a Spanish-speaking fan in Buenos Aires and one in Madrid may use different words for the same part. Good motorsport translation starts by knowing who is reading.

Based on public information about the 2027 Formula 2 car.
